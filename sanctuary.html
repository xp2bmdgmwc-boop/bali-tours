Sacred Water Sanctuary Tegallalang · 650 m

Pura Gunung Kawi Sebatu & Водный Каньон

Hidden 11th-century water sanctuary & secluded canyon Melukat springs

Unlike commercialized tourist sites, Sebatu is an intimate 11th-century sanctuary nestled in a verdant jungle gorge, dedicated to Lord Vishnu and Dewi Danu, guardian of freshwater. Its focal point is an 800 m² natural spring pool of luminescent turquoise water where sacred koi drift around ornate Bale pavilions. Tucked deeper down the river canyon is the secluded Genah Melukat waterfall, fed by subterranean volcanic springs. The private Melukat purification guided by a hereditary Mangku priest unfolds in serene stillness, effortlessly dissolving emotional tension and restoring vibrant inner vitality.

Valeriy's Insight: “The spring water in Sebatu has an extraordinary tactile clarity: it washes away months of sensory overload. Guests emerge with effortless mental stillness and an inner glow.”
The Mother Temple Mount Agung · 1 000 m

Pura Agung Besakih

The cosmic Axis Mundi on Mount Agung: 2,000 years of unbroken sacred lineage

Bali’s supreme mother temple spanning over 150,000 m² at 1,000 meters altitude on the sacred stratovolcano Mount Agung (3,142 m). Megalithic stepped terraces here predate Hinduism by two millennia. The complex unites over 86 shrines across rising terraces. A ceremonial stairway of 52 stone steps ascends through split Candi Bentar gates to Pura Agung Penataran, home to an open 24-pillar pavilion and the Padmasana lotus throne of the Trimurti: Shiva (white), Brahma (red), and Vishnu (black), beneath the yellow canopy of supreme creation. Tiered Meru shrines thatched with black sugar-palm fiber silhouette against mountain mists. In 1963, during the once-a-century Eka Dasa Rudra ritual, catastrophic lava flows from Mount Agung miraculously parted around Besakih's walls, leaving the sanctum untouched.

Valeriy's Insight: “Balinese sleep with their heads toward Mount Agung to align with pure cosmic energy. We arrive late in the afternoon when day visitors disperse, watching cool mountain fog drift between ancient Meru pagodas.”
Rainforest Sanctuary Tabanan · 820 m

Pura Luhur Batukaru

Sanctuary of Mahadewa deep in virgin mountain cloud forests

One of Bali’s six paramount directional protector sanctuaries (Sad Kahyangan), guarding the island from the west. Hidden on the misty flanks of Mount Batukaru (2,276 m) amidst ancient rainforest where velvet moss carpets towering hardwood trees and wild orchids. Completely devoid of commercial vendors, it centers around a sacred spring-fed lotus lake and a revered 7-tiered Meru honoring Mahadewa. The atmosphere breathes deep stillness, mountain rain, and cedar incense.

Valeriy's Insight: “Batukaru gently recalibrates your nervous system. The cool silence of primary rainforest grounds and restores a profound sense of inner safety.”

Highland Lake Sanctuary Bedugul · 1 250–1 450 m

Озеро Братан, Pura Ulun Danu & Сады Бедугула

Sacred lake temple of goddess Dewi Danu, 157 hectares of botanical forest & orchid houses

A crisp highland sanctuary (+18...+22°C) nestled within the ancient volcanic caldera of Bedugul. Pura Ulun Danu Beratan, built in 1633 by the King of Mengwi, appears to float magically upon the mirrored surface of Lake Beratan — the sacred water temple dedicated to goddess Dewi Danu who blesses Bali’s ancient Subak irrigation system. Nearby spans Indonesia’s grandest botanical reserve, Kebun Raya Bali (157 hectares): avenues of towering conifers, Jurassic tree ferns, and conservatories of rare wild orchids. The route can also be tailored to include the private Bali Bird Park showcasing elusive birds of paradise and sacred Bali starlings. The mountain air is crystalline and pure — an ideal setting for deep meditative breaths.

Valeriy's Insight: “Morning mist gently lifting from Lake Beratan with the tiered shrines of Ulun Danu floating in silence is one of earth's most calming sights. The crisp mountain air instantly refreshes the senses from the tropical heat below.”
Artistic Heritage Ubud & Campuhan

Музеи Убуда: Blanco Renaissance, Neka & ARMA

Intimate sanctuaries of Balinese avant-garde, surrealism & sacred keris daggers

Ubud’s supreme artistic triptych revealing Bali as an international nexus of visual culture. The Blanco Renaissance Museum — the hill palace of Catalan eccentric Antonio Blanco (“the Salvador Dalí of Bali”) and his son Mario perched above the sacred Campuhan river junction: sensual baroque portraiture, hand-carved sculptural frames, and gardens inhabited by macaw parrots and hornbills. The Neka Art Museum — Suteja Neka’s encyclopedic archive tracing a century of Balinese painting (from ancient Kamasan scrolls to 1930s Pita Maha masters and Arie Smit), crowned by the revered Keris Pavilion preserving sacred meteoric daggers infused with ancestral spirit (tuah). The ARMA Museum (Agung Rai) — an expansive estate set amidst working rice paddies housing the island's only original masterworks by Walter Spies and Javanese romantic master Raden Saleh.

Valeriy's Insight: “These are not static European galleries. Art here breathes within tropical foliage, jasmine air, and distant gamelan chords. The Keris Pavilion at Neka is profoundly magnetic — blades hand-folded with meteoric nickel resonate with immense ancestral power.”
